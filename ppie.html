Patient and Public Involvement and Engagement (PPIE) has been embedded across the Health Research from Home programme, and is led jointly by patient partner Lynn Laidlaw, and academic rheumatologist Charlotte Sharp. We hope you will find the resources that we have developed with our patient and public contributors useful when considering how and when you might work with patients and the public in smartphones and wearables research.

Taking part, testing or shaping research?

What’s the difference between taking part in a study, testing an app or device, helping shape the research, and discussing it with a wider audience? This short animation explains these roles and how each contribution is used in research involving smartphones and wearables.

Talking Heads: PPIE across the research lifecycle

In these short films, we explore how patients and members of the public can help shape research using smartphones and wearables, from deciding what to study to sharing what was found. Select a stage on the research lifecycle graphic to watch the film, or start with our Top Tips for practical tips for working together throughout a study.

Video 1: Starting with the right question See how early conversations with patients and the public can help researchers identify what matters to people and decide which questions to ask. Watch the video
Video 2: Designing the study Explore how patients and public contributors can help design a study that fits into people’s everyday lives, including what they’re asked to do with an app or wearable. Watch the video
Video 3: Setting up the study and preparing for delivery See how public contributors can help make study information and instructions clearer and flag practical problems before recruitment begins.
Video 4: Running the study Find out how patients and public contributors can help the team respond to issues with recruitment, technology or participant support once the study is underway. Watch the video
Video 5: Making sense of the findings Explore how lived experience can help researchers interpret patterns in the data and consider what might explain gaps or unexpected findings. Watch the video
Video 6: Sharing findings and planning what comes next See how public contributors can help decide what to share, how to feed back to participants and which questions to explore next. Watch the video
